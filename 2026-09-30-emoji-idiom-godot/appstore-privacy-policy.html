Privacy Policy

Emoji Guessing Fun · iOS
Effective and updated: October 12, 2026

Emoji Guessing Fun is an emoji-based puzzle game with Chinese word and cultural themes. This policy explains data practices for the app and support provided by Yufang Wu.

You can play without creating an account. Puzzle progress, earned coins, favorites, daily challenge history, language, audio preferences, review-request records, and advertising cooldown records are stored on your device. The game does not provide cloud saves, social sign-in, chat, or in-app purchases.

Game data on your device

We do not operate a server that receives your game progress or answers. The game stores progress and settings in its local app storage. Your operating system may handle backups separately under its own settings.

Reset Progress clears progress, coins, favorites, and daily challenge history. Audio and language preferences and a review-request record may remain after a reset. To remove all local app data, use your device's app-data controls or uninstall the app. A system backup may restore previously saved data.

Advertising and consent on iOS

The app uses Google AdMob for optional rewarded hint ads and interstitial ads at level-end pauses, and Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) for consent choices. Advertisements are not required to enter the game. A hint ad is offered through a choice that you can cancel.

Google's advertising services may receive device or app identifiers, IP-derived approximate location, advertising and app interactions, and crash or performance diagnostics. These services use such information to deliver and measure ads, operate consent features, analyze performance, and reduce fraud.

Before relevant advertising requests, the app asks for Apple's App Tracking Transparency permission when applicable and updates UMP consent information. The app initializes and requests ads only when UMP permits requests. Permission and consent choices affect which identifiers and advertising features are available; refusing tracking permission does not prevent you from playing.

You can manage tracking permission in iOS Settings. When UMP requires a privacy-options entry, it is available in the game's Settings. Changing tracking or consent choices does not erase locally saved puzzle progress.

Device access

The game plays background music and sound effects but does not record audio. It does not request access to the camera, microphone, photo library, contacts, or precise location. Advertising services may infer a broad location from an IP address without a location-permission request.

Support and your choices

If you email us, we receive your email address and the information you choose to include so that we can answer your request. You do not need to send a password, account credentials, or sensitive personal information. Support correspondence is handled separately from the game's local save data.

You may contact us about access, correction, or deletion of information we control, such as support correspondence. Data handled by Google is also subject to Google's privacy policy, retention practices, and privacy controls. We do not promise to erase data held independently by Google through a game-progress reset.

Retention and security

Local save data remains until you reset it, remove the app's data, or uninstall the app, subject to operating-system backups. We keep support correspondence only as needed to respond and meet applicable obligations. Google's SDK controls retention of advertising and consent information. Google states that its advertising SDK uses encrypted connections for data transmission.

Children and guardians

The game does not ask players to provide an age or date of birth. Parents and guardians should review the platform's age rating and supervise children's use, including advertising and consent choices. Please contact us if you believe a child's personal information has been provided to us.

Third-party services and external links

Advertising and consent services are provided by Google. Opening a store review page, a policy page, or an email link takes you to a service governed by its own terms and privacy practices.
